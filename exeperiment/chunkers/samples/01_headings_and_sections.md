# Product handbook

The product handbook introduces a fictional document review service. Each team
uses the same vocabulary, but different sections describe different jobs.
Chunks should retain enough heading context to distinguish those jobs.

## Getting started

A reviewer receives a workspace invitation and opens the document queue.
The queue shows the document title, submission time, and current owner.
Reading a document does not change its review status.

### First review

Read the summary before inspecting the supporting evidence. Record a question
when a statement cannot be verified from the supplied material. Keep the
question attached to the document rather than sending an unrelated message.

### Returning to a review

The activity log records the last completed step. A reviewer can continue from
that step without repeating the initial inspection. Earlier comments remain
visible so that decisions have an explanation.

## Administration

Administrators manage workspace access and naming conventions. They do not
approve a document merely because they can edit workspace settings.

### Workspace roles

Readers inspect published material. Reviewers annotate drafts. Administrators
manage membership. These responsibilities remain separate when a person has
more than one role.

### Naming conventions

Use a short descriptive title and a stable document identifier. Updating a title
does not create a new document. Moving a document to a different collection
does not erase its history.

## Support

Include the document identifier and the action that failed when requesting
support. Do not include passwords, access tokens, or private attachments.

## Operational casebook

### Case 001: Revision identity

#### Evidence owned by Editorial

For packet DOC-1001, the Editorial team examines revision identity. The intake record reports that two drafts share a title but have different revision dates. Available evidence includes a signed revision log and the original submission receipt. The assigned reviewer must compare the stable identifier before accepting the newer draft. The intended outcome is that the original submission remains traceable after the title changes. The decision note records the inspected revision, the unresolved assumption, and the next responsible person. A later reviewer should be able to distinguish the original observation from the interpretation without reconstructing an undocumented conversation.

#### Handoff condition

The next owner checks whether the original submission remains traceable after the title changes. If that condition cannot be established, the request remains open and the Editorial team identifies the evidence still needed. Changing a display title does not authorize a different conclusion.
